For hackclub week 1, i've made a flight controller for a rocket. It handles 4 pyro channels (2 on top, 2 on the bottom). It has an stm32 for the processor, as well as several other sensors such as barometric, altitude, gps, radio, etc. It recieves power from a 2s lipo. Everything was built around breakout boards, so if the rocket experiences any rough landing or the chips just break in general, it should be easy to replace and won't brick the entire board if it does break.

======================================

The stm32 acts as the main computer. I would have used a teensy, but that would have cost way too much. Pins A8, B2, B10, and C15 are hooked up to mosfets, which then link to the pyro channels. Linking them directly would kill the stm32, so the mosfet acts as a mediator. Through headers, the Ms5611 (pressure sensor to measure barometric data at the top of the ascent), GPS module, LoRa module (transmitting data directly down to a computer) and the other sensors are soldered to the headers and are more or less directly linked to the pins on the STM32. Power on the other hand, goes through a p-mosfet, buck regulator (step down), capacitors to smooth out voltage and keep everything safe, as well as a bunch of diodes and resistors.
